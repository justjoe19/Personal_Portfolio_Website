When someone in Granger searches "furnace repair near me" or someone in Elkhart searches "roof leak repair," Google shows two things: a map with three businesses (the "local pack") and a list of websites underneath. If your business isn't in either spot, that customer is calling someone else.

Google says local results are based on three things: **relevance** (does your business match the search?), **distance** (how close are you to the person searching?), and **prominence** (how well known and trusted are you?). You can't move your shop closer to every customer, but you have a lot of control over relevance and prominence.

These are the three mistakes I see most often from small businesses across Michiana — and each one is fixable.

## Mistake 1: Treating your Google Business Profile as "set it and forget it"

Your [Google Business Profile](https://www.google.com/business/) is the listing that appears in the map results. For most local service businesses, it influences more calls than any other single thing online. Yet many profiles were set up years ago and never touched again.

**Common problems:**

- The wrong primary category (for example, "Contractor" instead of "Roofing contractor")
- Missing services, service areas, or business hours
- No recent photos, or only a logo
- Old or inconsistent phone numbers

**The fix:**

1. Choose the most specific primary category that fits your core service, and add secondary categories for the rest.
2. List every service you offer and every town you serve.
3. Add real photos of your team, trucks, and finished jobs regularly.
4. Make sure your business name, address, and phone number match your website exactly.

## Mistake 2: One "Services" page trying to rank for everything

Here's a pattern I see constantly: a plumbing company does drain cleaning, water heater installs, sewer line repair, and emergency service — and the website has one page called "Services" with a bulleted list.

Google ranks *pages*, not websites. A single page can't be the best answer for "water heater installation Mishawaka" *and* "emergency plumber South Bend" at the same time.

**The fix:** give each core service its own page, and write it for a real customer:

- What the service is and when someone needs it
- What the process looks like and how long it takes
- Photos from real jobs
- The towns you serve for that service
- A clear way to call or request a quote

For businesses that serve several towns, a dedicated page for each main service area helps too — as long as each one says something genuinely useful about working in that area, rather than the same text with the city name swapped.

## Mistake 3: Collecting reviews, but not using them

Reviews are one of the clearest prominence signals Google has, and they matter just as much to the customer deciding who to call. Most businesses know they need reviews. Fewer do anything with them after they arrive.

**Common problems:**

- Only asking for reviews occasionally, so they come in bursts
- Never responding, especially to negative reviews
- Reviews living only on Google, never shown on the website

**The fix:**

1. **Ask every happy customer**, right after the job, with a direct link to your review page.
2. **Respond to every review** — thank the positive ones and address the negative ones calmly and specifically. Future customers read your responses. (If replying feels like a chore, try my free [AI Review Responder](/review-responder) to draft a reply in seconds.)
3. **Show reviews on your website** next to the services they're about, so visitors see proof right where they're deciding.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does local SEO take to work?

Google Business Profile changes can show results within a few weeks. New service and location pages usually take a few months to reach their full ranking potential, depending on how competitive your trade and area are.

### Do I need to pay for ads to show up on Google Maps?

No. The regular map results are organic. Ads can appear above them, but a well-maintained profile, strong reviews, and a solid website can earn you a spot without paying per click.

### Do I need a physical office in every town I serve?

No. Service-area businesses can list the towns they serve without an address in each one. Service-area pages on your website help reinforce where you work.
