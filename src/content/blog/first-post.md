If you run a small business in South Bend, Mishawaka, or Elkhart and you're planning a new website, the first real decision isn't the color scheme. It's the platform. That choice determines how fast your site loads, how much upkeep it needs, how well it can rank in local search, and what it will cost you over the next five years.

There are three realistic options for most local businesses: a DIY site builder (Wix, Squarespace, GoDaddy), WordPress, or a custom-built website. Here's an honest look at each — including when a custom site is *not* the right call.

## Option 1: DIY site builders (Wix, Squarespace, GoDaddy)

Site builders let you drag and drop your way to a website in a weekend, and for some businesses that's exactly right.

**Good for:** brand-new businesses testing an idea, very small budgets, and owners who enjoy editing their own site.

**Watch out for:**

- **Monthly fees forever.** You rent the platform; you never own the site. If you leave, you usually start over.
- **Performance ceilings.** Builders load code for every feature they offer, whether you use it or not. It's hard to get a builder site to score well on Google's Core Web Vitals, especially on phones.
- **Generic structure.** Service businesses need specific pages — one per service, one per town you serve — and builders make that tedious to do well.

## Option 2: WordPress

WordPress runs a huge share of the web, and it's a reasonable choice when you have someone who will maintain it.

**Good for:** businesses that publish a lot of content themselves and have a developer or agency keeping things updated.

**Watch out for:**

- **Plugin sprawl.** Most WordPress sites rely on a stack of plugins — one for forms, one for SEO, one for galleries, one for caching. Each one adds weight and another thing that can break or conflict.
- **Constant updates.** WordPress core, the theme, and every plugin need regular security updates. Skipped updates are one of the most common ways small-business sites get hacked. (I cover this in more detail in [why South Bend businesses are rethinking website security](/blog/the-headless-advantage-why-south-bend-businesses-are-rethinking-website-secur/).)
- **Page-builder bloat.** Many WordPress themes use visual page builders that generate heavy code, which slows the site down on mobile.

## Option 3: A custom-built website

A custom site is designed and coded specifically for your business. The sites I build use modern tools like [Astro](https://astro.build) and React, and are compiled into lightweight, static pages before they're ever served to a visitor.

**Good for:** service businesses that depend on their website for leads, companies competing hard in local search, and owners who'd rather not manage software updates.

**What you get:**

- **Speed by default.** A page only ships the code it needs. There's no plugin stack and no database lookup on every visit, so pages load quickly even on a weak cell signal.
- **A smaller attack surface.** With no admin login or plugin ecosystem exposed on the public site, most of the automated attacks aimed at small-business sites simply have nothing to target.
- **Structure built for local SEO.** Service pages, city pages, schema markup, and click-to-call buttons are part of the plan from day one instead of add-ons.
- **Ownership.** You own the finished site and its content.

**The trade-off:** a custom site costs more up front than a template, and you'll typically make content changes through your developer rather than dragging blocks around yourself. ## Which one should you choose?

A simple way to decide:

1. **If your website rarely brings in business** and you just need something online, a site builder is fine.
2. **If you publish content constantly** and have a developer on call, WordPress can work well.
3. **If your phone ringing depends on your website** — HVAC, roofing, plumbing, restoration, and other field-service trades — a fast, custom site is usually worth the investment, because every missed call is a job that went to a competitor.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a custom website more expensive than WordPress?

Usually up front, yes. Over several years the gap narrows, because you're not paying for premium plugins, theme licenses, or emergency fixes after a bad update. ### Can I switch from WordPress to a custom site without losing my Google rankings?

Yes, if the move is planned. The key steps are keeping (or properly redirecting) your existing URLs, carrying over your page titles and content, and resubmitting your sitemap to Google Search Console.

### Can I still update my own content on a custom site?

It depends on the build. Some sites include a simple content editor for things like blog posts. For everything else, small text and photo changes are included in my [$100/month hosting and maintenance plan](/#hosting).
